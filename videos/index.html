What the first walkthrough covers

SKSurrogate does not search a grid and return a winner. It fits a response surface to the evaluations it has spent, uses that surface to choose the next candidate, and keeps every evaluation on the record. The video follows that loop through an actual pipeline:

  • Composition. Data transformers and scikit-learn estimators assembled into candidate pipelines, so the search space is pipeline structure, not only hyperparameters.
  • Search. Surrogate-based modelling paired with evolutionary search (EOA) to move through a combinatorial space that is far too large to enumerate.
  • Preprocessing discipline. Fold-local preprocessing and out-of-fold stacking — fitted inside each fold, never on the full dataset.
  • Evaluation. Nested cross-validation, learning curves and interpretability read-outs, which is what separates a measured result from a lucky one.
  • The artifact. A registered bundle carrying its data identity and evaluation record, not just a fitted object.

What it is not

A benchmark result. The numbers that decide whether this beats a given baseline live on the benchmark page, at equal evaluation budgets, including the cells where the surrogate loses — and the honest conclusion there is that the response surface matters more than the search mechanism.
